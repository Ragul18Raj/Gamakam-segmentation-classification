# Tonic for Gamaka_veena_dataset

`tonic_estimates.csv` assigns one fixed tonic to all 222 recordings:

**Sa = E4 = 329.3 Hz**, method `fixed_dataset_tonic_E4_corroborated`

**Status: strong inference from audio statistics. This is not a confirmed ground-truth tonic.**
No tonic metadata exists for this dataset. One way to turn this into fact is to ask the
performer (Aruna) whether Sa = E on this instrument.

## Why one dataset-wide tonic

Three independent lines of evidence point to the same answer:

1. **Dataset-wide histogram peak.** Across all 222 clips, E is the most common held pitch:
   37.6% of high-confidence, sustained CREPE frames (confidence ≥ 0.8, pitch change < 20
   cents between neighbouring frames). The next most common is B at 16.7%, which is Pa
   if Sa = E. F (R1) is only 0.2%.
2. **Every session lands on a note of the E major scale.** Each session's most common held
   pitch is a note of the E major scale (E F# G# A B C# D#), except one session that lands
   on C. See `session_tonics.csv`, column `mode_swara`. This is what you would expect from
   one instrument, tuned once, used to demonstrate different scale degrees.
3. **Filename cross-check.** `odukkul (kambhoji) pa dha sa` has pooled peaks at B3, C#4 and
   E4, which is Pa, Dha, Sa if Sa = E. The filename was written independently of the
   histogram, so this is not circular.

## Why not per-clip or per-session estimates

A histogram mode is the note held longest, which is not necessarily Sa. For example,
andolita (shankrabarnam) clips 1–3 peak at 209 Hz (G#, Ga) and clips 4–6 peak at 248.8 Hz
(B, Pa). The pooled session estimate (248.8 Hz) is the demonstrated note, not the tonic.
Pooling clips cannot fix a session that spends more time on another note than on Sa.
`session_tonics.csv` keeps these per-session results as supporting evidence only. Its
`pooled_mode_hz` column is **not** a tonic.

## Octave

E4 (329.3 Hz, 28.7% of sustained frames) was picked over E3 (164.8 Hz, 7.8%) only because
it is the more common octave in the data. `anchor_feasibility_check.py` folds pitches into
one octave (`% 1200` in `swara_distance()`), so E3 and E4 give identical results.
